How to Find Devices Connected to Your Wi-Fi Network

Learn how to check devices on your Wi-Fi, understand signal strength and connection speed, and know when to review your router.

If your home Wi-Fi seems slow or you want to check which devices are using it, start with the device list in your router settings. A network-scanning app can provide a quick overview, but its results need context and do not prove that an unknown device is an intruder.

How to check devices connected to Wi-Fi

First, connect your phone to the same Wi-Fi network you want to inspect. Open your router’s administration page and look for a connected-devices or active-clients list. This is the most useful place to verify what the router currently sees.

You can also run a local network scan on your phone to get an initial list of devices that respond during the scan. Compare the results with your phones and smart-home devices, then check the router if a device remains unfamiliar. Names may be generic, so do not identify a device by its displayed name alone.

Why might a scan not match your device list?

A device may appear under a generic name or an address that does not identify it clearly. A sleeping or disconnected device may not respond at the time of the scan. Guest networks and client-isolation settings can also affect what your phone can discover. Treat scan results as a prompt to investigate, not as a definitive list or security report.

Wi-Fi signal strength is not the same as internet speed

Signal strength describes the wireless connection between your phone and router where you are standing. Internet speed is also affected by your service plan, network congestion, other devices, and the test service. A stronger signal does not necessarily mean your provider is delivering more bandwidth, and one test is not enough to judge the connection.

For a useful comparison, test from the same location at different times and pause large downloads. If reception is weak in one room, move closer to the router and compare. If the connection remains slow everywhere, review router settings or contact your internet provider.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Wi-Fi scan identify the owner of every device?

No. Scan results may help you distinguish devices, but names can be generic or unclear. Compare the router’s device list with the devices you own before taking action.

Does a network-scanning app make internet faster?

A scan does not change your internet plan or increase its speed. It helps you review signal, devices, and connection readings so you can decide what to check next.

Does an unfamiliar device mean my Wi-Fi was hacked?

Not necessarily. It may be a device in your home with an unclear name or a guest device. Check the router and your own devices; if you confirm unauthorized access, change your Wi-Fi password in the router settings.

In short: device scans and signal analysis help you understand a Wi-Fi network, but the router is the place to verify device access and manage connections. Readings alone cannot guarantee faster internet.
